## Wi-Fi (802.11)

### Naming
- Official IEEE standards use the 802.11 naming (e.g., 802.11ac, 802.11ax, 802.11be)
- Consumer-friendly names:
  - **Wi-Fi 5** = 802.11ac
  - **Wi-Fi 6 / Wi-Fi 6E** = 802.11ax
  - **Wi-Fi 7** = 802.11be

### Frequency Bands
| Band        | Characteristics                              | Common Use                  |
|-------------|----------------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **2.4 GHz** | Longer range, more interference, fewer channels | Older devices, IoT         |
| **5 GHz**   | Faster speeds, less interference, shorter range | Modern Wi-Fi               |
| **6 GHz**   | Newest, cleanest spectrum, highest speeds    | Wi-Fi 6E and Wi-Fi 7       |

### Channels & Bandwidth
- Frequencies are grouped into **channels** (much easier to remember than exact GHz values)
- Channel bandwidth options: **20 MHz, 40 MHz, 80 MHz, 160 MHz**
- Wider channels = higher throughput (but fewer available channels)

**2.4 GHz**:
- Limited spectrum
- Only **3 non-overlapping 20 MHz channels** (1, 6, 11)
- High chance of interference

**5 GHz & 6 GHz**:
- Much more spectrum available
- Support for wider channels (40/80/160 MHz)
- Less interference → better performance

## Bluetooth
- Uses the **2.4 GHz** ISM band (Industrial, Scientific, Medical – unlicensed)
- Designed for **Personal Area Networks (PAN)**
- Typical range: ~**10 meters** (consumer devices)
- Common uses: headsets, speakers, keyboards, mice, smartwatches

---

## RFID (Radio Frequency Identification)
- One-way communication technology
- Tags can be:
  - **Passive** (no battery – powered by the reader’s radio signal)
  - **Active** (have a battery – longer range)
- Very common uses:
  - Access control badges
  - Inventory / product tracking
  - Pet microchips
  - Assembly line tracking
- Tags can be extremely small (size of a grain of rice)

---

## NFC (Near Field Communication)
- Built on RFID technology
- Supports **two-way communication**
- Extremely short range (usually a few centimeters)
- Popular uses:
  - Contactless payments (phone / smartwatch)
  - Quick device pairing (Bluetooth or Wi-Fi setup)
  - Access control / identification
  - Data transfer between phones

---

## Key Takeaways
- Wi-Fi naming has been simplified (Wi-Fi 5 / 6 / 6E / 7)
- 2.4 GHz is crowded → prefer 5 GHz or 6 GHz when possible
- Channel selection is important to avoid interference
- Bluetooth = short-range personal devices
- RFID = identification & tracking (mostly one-way)
- NFC = very short-range, two-way communication (payments, pairing)
